MesserBase

Personal Defense Weapons of the 15th and 16th Centuries

About this database

MesserBase is intended as a community project for those interested in historical fencing, in particular those interested in the family of weapons broadly referred to as Messer.

Their most common historical context was that of the everyday-carry weapon of civilians in the Late Middle Ages throughout most of Europe, from Iceland to Ukraine. The development of each category of Messer is still largely understudied. Check the "Categories" and "Types" tabs for a brief summary of what is currently known.

The goal of MesserBase is to provide a central hub for the disparate datasets of original Messer recorded by various members of this community, as well as published examples and even ones just spotted in museum exhibitions or in a museum's online database.

Whether historical fencer, smith, backyard cutter or academic, all wish to base their opinions and actions on an accurate picture of the past. For this, a good understanding of the source material is necessary. Though great advances in our understanding have been made in recent years, I hope an overview such as this will aid personal or academic study of the various aspects of these objects. (Not least, my own study of them.)

Built with GitHub Copilot by Bjarne Kortmann, archaeologist and historical fencer from Berlin, MesserBase benefited greatly from early feedback by James G. Elmslie and Falko Fritz.

Categories

Messer are commonly categorized into three to four main variants, based on size and construction. For this, partly historical and partly modern terms are used by museums and authors. Some examples are inevitably going to be difficult to assign to one category or another or feature one or two aspects uncharacteristic of their category.

MesserBase currently differentiates between: Bauernwehr, Langes Messer, Grosses Messer and Kriegsmesser.

The way these are defined and named here may be new to many, but I hope most visitors will agree with the logic and find it helpful in quickly understanding what kind of object they are looking at. Additional categories may be added in the future for regional variants known under their own names.

Bauernwehr

A Messer with a protective, often flattened Nagel (nail) between hand and blade. It protects the hand from sliding onto the blade and perhaps also from opposing blades. A Bauernwehr does not feature a sword-like crossguard, but it can feature a metal guard that offers an additional fingerstop above the index finger. Usually single-edged, but many possess a short edge (often called false edge) on their back, sometimes in order to assist in thrusting, and perhaps to enable back-edge cuts. They can span the whole spectrum from cut-optimized, cut-and-thrust to thrust-optimized. Those optimized for the thrust are sometimes known as Rugger.

tl;dr Think of daggers, bowie knives and short machetes.

Langes Messer

Larger version of the Bauernwehr that now features a crossguard, protecting the hand along three axes together with the nail. The crossguard and their size allow them to be used much like one-handed swords. This is the only category covered by fencing manuscripts, thus being the one receiving the most interest from historical fencers. Most commonly existed as a cut-and-thrust weapon, though some examples leaned towards the thrust, while others were more curved and favored the cut. Many featured a dedicated short edge on the back, assisting in thrusts and enabling back edge cuts. Sometimes the grip is long enough to be used two-handed, though this is unattested in fencing literature. A large variety of blade, guard and hilt design on surviving examples and even more in art. Possibly the category that was the most widespread geographically and chronologically, with many variants and descendants.

tl;dr Think of a one-handed sword in the style of a bowie knife.

Grosses Messer

A larger version of the Langes Messer that can still be comfortably worn at the hip, but is more comfortably wielded with two hands or from horseback. After Messer had become popular with the highest strata of society, these were worn by emperors and kings. Due to many of them featuring costly hilt designs, representing their wearers’ status, comparatively many have been preserved in excellent condition in collections and museums, despite most likely being less numerous and more short-lived than the Langes Messer in period. Even among those examples that have a blade well-suited for thrusts, the presence of a short edge seems to be slightly rarer than on the Langes Messer. The term Grosses Messer is often used synonymously with Langes Messer or Kriegsmesser by others.

tl;dr Think of a bastard sword/hand-and-a-half sword/longsword, but sometimes single-edged and sometimes curved.

Kriegsmesser

Largest version of the Messer family. No longer easily carried on the hip or maneuvered indoors, this is the version most commonly depicted on the shoulders of Landsknechte or other soldiers in battles or parades. Designs are often stouter and plainer than those of the Grosse Messer and almost always feature a curved blade optimized for cutting. They sometimes feature almost comically long grips in order to provide sufficient leverage. The term Kriegsmesser is often employed for any two-handed Messer by others.

tl;dr Think of a greatsword/claymore/montante/Zweihänder though more optimized for the cut. A giant machete with a crossguard and nail.

How to contribute data

In order to avoid fees and for reasons of simplicity and longevity, MesserBase is hosted as a static website by GitHub. Due to this, it is not possible to enter new data by visitors of the database.

If you would like to add objects to the database, or add data to existing objects, please send an Email to bjarne.kortmann@twerchhau.de with information of what you would like to add. The relevant data can already be attached to your mail or I can provide you with an upload link.

The contributed data can be in any format you have it in, from pencil notes to 3D scans. I will order the data to fit the database. To assist the data entry, you can – but do not have to – submit your data according to the following format:

  • Name — Object name, such as an inventory number.
  • Alternate name — For cases in which objects are known by other, informal names.
  • Category (category) — Object category, see "Categories" tab for viable options.
  • Type (type) — Specific type, see "Types" tab for viable options.
  • Date — Earliest and latest estimated years, in numbers.
  • Regional origin — Region where the object most likely originated, if known.
  • Current location — Where the object is held, such as a Museum or private collection.
  • References — Link to a museum's object database or title of a publication etc.
  • Description — Brief object description, not standardized. Whatever you would like to mention about the object that there are no matching fields for. Feel free to add tags to be found by the description search.
  • Weight — Object weight displayed in g, as preserved.
  • Point of balance — Most commonly measured from the guard, but other methods can be specified. Displayed in cm.
  • Length — Overall length, displayed in cm.
  • Blade length — Length of the blade, tip to guard, displayed in cm.
  • False edge length — Length of the false edge, displayed in cm. Additional information or absence can be noted.
  • Blade width — Blade width, displayed in cm. Multiple measurements possible (and desired).
  • Crossguard length — Length of the crossguard, displayed in cm.
  • Nail/ring depth — Depth of the nail or ring, displayed in cm.
  • Distal taper (distal taper) — Blade thickness along its length, displayed in mm. Multiple measurements possible (and desired).
  • Grip (grip) — Grip dimensions displayed in cm. Details regarding shape or construction can be specified.
  • Pommel/cap dimensions — Pommel or end-cap dimensions, displayed in cm. Additional details can be specified.
  • Contributor — Data or image contributor, if you or your institution would like to be mentioned.

Images (photos or drawings) and even 3D models can accompany each object. If you can provide Images, please inform me about their copyright status and whether further use of them is permitted or not.
